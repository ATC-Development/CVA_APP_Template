Open decision
Can a supervisor assign a standalone action item with no source record? This prototype says yes — it is how real work gets tracked. The cost is that it becomes possible to build a documentation trail on an employee outside any conversation they were part of. Mitigation implemented here: standalone items are always visible to the employee immediately. There is no such thing as a hidden action item.

The metric that matters
Not how many growth requests are approved — how many get a straight answer. A declined request answered in three days with a written reason is a healthier signal than an approved one that took six weeks. Silence is the only real failure, which is why the SLA escalates rather than the approval rate being targeted.

Response
Two controls this screen implements deliberately
  • Decline requires a written reason. The button is disabled with an empty reason field, and the API rejects the transition regardless of what the client sends. This is the "own your work, sign your name to it" value made structural — a supervisor may say no, but not anonymously and not without saying why.
  • Acknowledgment is separate from decision. Acknowledging within 5 business days costs nothing and buys the employee the knowledge that they were heard. Deciding can legitimately take longer. Conflating the two is what turns this feature into a black hole.
Third question still open, and it is the one that most needs HR: can an employee route a growth request past their supervisor, directly to HR? This prototype does not allow it. That is a defensible default and a bad answer in the specific case where the supervisor is the obstacle.
