Employee Performance Record · UI Prototypes

Design references for the ground-up rebuild of ATC's performance program (React PWA + ASP.NET Core API). The scope now spans six record types on one employee record: CVA (annual, required), PPM (mid-cycle), supervisor notes, one-on-ones, action items, and employee-initiated growth requests. These are single-file HTML mockups with sample data — they define layout, interaction, and visual language, and they exist to force the open decisions listed on each screen. Nothing here is connected to a data source and nothing entered is saved.

Scope note — 01–04 vs 05–07. Prototypes 01 through 04 cover the locked CVA and PPM foundation. Prototypes 05 through 07 are the proposed expansion into a continuous employee record. They are exploratory: the entities they imply (OneOnOne, ActionItem, GrowthRequest, and the EmployeeTimeline read model) are not in CVA_Architecture_And_Data_Model.md v3.1 and are not committed. Each of the three carries a design-note panel naming the decisions it forces.

PPM Form Screens

PPM_Form_Screens.html

The Performance Planning Meeting lifecycle (Phase 2). Five modes on a top switcher: Employee Drafting and Supervisor Drafting (same dockable widget workspace — six-question form with prior-plan chips, last PPM both sides, last CVA, supervisor notes with AI assist), Ready to Meet (both sides sealed until the supervisor releases the merge), Merged Record (two-column, both signatures), and Complete (locked; HR visibility begins here).

  • Dual independent sides
  • Prior-plan chips (A↔D · B↔E · C↔F)
  • Goal items, not blobs
  • Supervisor-released merge
  • No ratings
  • HR sees Complete only

05

Employee Record · Timeline

Employee_Record.html

The proposed spine. One chronological record per employee, projecting every record type — CVA, PPM, supervisor notes, one-on-ones, action items, growth requests — through a single visibility predicate. Four viewer hats: Employee, Supervisor, Chain (Area Manager, via transitiveReports), and HR Operations. Switching hats re-runs the predicate and shows what each role is withheld.

  • Read model, not an aggregate
  • Per-hat visibility
  • Notes excluded from the chain
  • PPM hidden from HR pre-Complete
  • Action items inherit their source gate

One-on-One Screens

OneOnOne_Screens.html

Documented one-on-ones as a shared, employee-acknowledged record. Five modes: Supervisor Drafting (with a private supervisor-note panel that writes to the notes log, not to this record), Employee Review (acknowledge ≠ agree, plus a permanent employee comment), Acknowledged & Locked, Chain & HR View, and a Cadence Tracker that makes the "does this become a metric" question explicit.

Action Items & Growth Requests

Action_Items.html

One polymorphic action item with a permanent SourceType, plus the employee-initiated growth request. Four hats: Mine, My Team (including the items the supervisor owes the employee), HR Operations (with source-gated rows shown as counts only), and the Growth Request lifecycle — where a decline is blocked without a written reason, server-side.

  • Polymorphic source
  • Supervisor-owned items in the same list
  • 5-day acknowledgment SLA
  • Decline requires a reason
  • No hidden action items
Open the action items prototype
Ground rules reflected in these prototypes: reviewers grade independently and cannot see each other's drafts until both are signed · AI assistance is suggest-only (category mapping and phrasing) and never assigns or implies a rating · HR notes live in their own table and are rendered alongside the form, never merged into comment text · every lifecycle transition is an explicit, signed human action · PPM sides are drafted independently and sealed until the supervisor releases the merge after the in-person meeting · the PPM has no ratings · HR has no visibility into a PPM before Complete.

Proposed additions under debate (05–07): the timeline is a projection and is never written to directly · supervisor notes stay with the current direct supervisor and HR and do not travel up the chain · a one-on-one draft is invisible to everyone but its author · a shared one-on-one is locked and corrected only by appended amendment · an action item inherits the visibility gate of whatever created it · standalone action items are visible to the employee immediately, so no action item is ever hidden from its owner · a growth request cannot be declined without a written reason, enforced at the API · acknowledgment and decision are separate obligations with separate clocks.
